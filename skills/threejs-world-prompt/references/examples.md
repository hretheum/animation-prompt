# Calibration examples

Three short, original example prompts that show the density and decisiveness a finished prompt should have. They are not templates to copy. Read only the one that matches the chosen form, take the level of detail from it, and write fresh content for the user's idea.

## 1. Looping show: "Tide pool at night"

**Form:** looping show, 45 seconds, seamless loop, no interaction required.

**Opening frame:** a hand-sized tide pool seen from straight above, black water, three lit anemones as the only light sources.

**Hero mechanic:** a slow wave of bioluminescence that travels from one anemone to the next, brightening the water as it passes. Each pass takes 9 seconds; the three anemones fire in a fixed rotation so the viewer learns the rhythm after one cycle.

**Style law:** deep teal and one warm amber accent, no pure white, soft volumetric glow, a faint caustic net on the pool floor. Procedural geometry only, no external assets.

**Camera:** a barely perceptible dolly-in over the first 20 seconds, then a slow pull back that returns exactly to the opening frame at 45 seconds.

**Acceptance criteria:** the loop point cannot be found by eye; the glow never clips to white; 60 fps on a mid-range laptop.

**Must keep:** the wave rhythm and the opening frame. **May sacrifice:** caustic detail, then glow resolution, in that order.

## 2. Physics toy: "Drawer of magnets"

**Form:** playground with physics, mouse or touch, runs indefinitely.

**Opening frame:** an open desk drawer filled with about 300 small magnetic spheres in muted brass, resting in a loose heap.

**Hero mechanic:** the cursor is a strong magnet. Hold to attract, release to let everything fall back. Spheres chain into stiff rods when they touch and snap apart with a click when pulled too hard.

**Style law:** warm studio light from the upper left, tactile brushed-metal material, shallow depth of field that blurs the back of the drawer.

**Feedback:** each snap triggers a short sound and a tiny spark at the contact point. The first interaction is invited by one sphere that twitches toward the cursor when the page loads.

**Acceptance criteria:** stable at 300 bodies with no jitter in the resting heap; a child can find the interaction in under three seconds.

**Must keep:** the chaining and the snap. **May sacrifice:** the depth-of-field blur and the sparks.

## 3. Page takeover: "Ink on the changelog"

**Form:** takeover of an existing documentation page by a visual effect, 20 seconds, then the page returns to normal.

**Opening frame:** the real page, untouched. After 2 seconds a single drop of ink lands on the first heading.

**Hero mechanic:** the ink spreads along the text lines, letter by letter, and turns each word it touches into a small flock of dark birds that lift off and cross the screen. Words not yet reached stay perfectly readable.

**Style law:** monochrome ink on the page's own background; the effect inherits the page's fonts and layout, nothing is replaced permanently.

**Ending:** the birds fly back and re-form the text in reading order; the final frame is pixel-identical to the first.

**Acceptance criteria:** the page remains selectable and scrollable throughout; layout never shifts; the effect can be disabled with one flag for reduced-motion users.

**Must keep:** the pixel-identical return. **May sacrifice:** flock size and the number of simultaneous birds.
